Math Fighter - ideas and three looks

Will: pictures only, nothing is built. Same scene in every picture: Lennon (his own skin and hair, from the picker) against the first champion, a question card on his side, the number pad under his thumb, health bars on top. Judge the look in Part 1, the ideas in Part 2. Mechanics borrowed from the genre only; no names, characters or art from any real game.

How a hit works. He gets a question. A right answer lands a punch and takes health off the champion. Fast right answers in a row build a COMBO bar; a full bar lets off a big special move. A wrong answer, or running out of time, is the champion hitting him. First to empty the other bar wins the round; best of three.

Part 2 — the ideas

Shown in look A. Each is a tick in the panel; they stack.

1

WORLD TOUR: one champion per kind of maths

Six champions, each one is a maths skill from his brain level and above. Tables Tiger (times tables) is first. Beat one and the next unlocks, and the questions get harder than his homework on purpose. Every win is a belt, and a belt is a card for his collection.

812 × 375 px
2

COMBO BAR and SPECIAL MOVE

Three fast right answers in a row fill the combo bar (the orange bar on the question card above). A full bar fires his special: one bigger question, worth triple damage. Miss it and the bar empties. This is the fighting-game feel, built from speed and accuracy instead of button mashing.

3

BLOCK: the champion winds up, he answers to block

When the champion attacks, a number appears over its fist and he has a few seconds to answer. Right in time is a block (no damage) and a counter-punch. Wrong or late and the punch lands. The timer gets shorter as his brain level climbs.

4

PICK YOUR POWER: easy, medium, hard question = small, medium, big hit

Before each punch he taps a difficulty. A harder sum does more damage. He chooses to climb past grade level because it wins faster. Nothing is forced; the game just pays better for harder maths.

5

TWO-PLAYER: you against Lennon on one iPad

Flat on the table, you each get your own side and your own number pad, facing you. Your questions are harder than his (17 × 12 against 7 × 8), so it is a fair fight. Same rules: answer to hit.
